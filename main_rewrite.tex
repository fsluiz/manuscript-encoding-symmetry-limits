\begin{abstract}
Which part of a Hamiltonian spectrum is physically relevant when both the
initial state and the interpolation preserve several symmetries?  We answer
this question for a compact multi-register encoding of Minimum Set Cover.
The represented symmetry is the direct product of register permutations
with the faithful base action of the incidence automorphism group.  We
distinguish its fixed, symmetry-allowed space from the generally smaller
cyclic space generated by the protocol, and define gaps relative to an
isolated band in that cyclic space.  A sector-resolved Schur-complement
bound certifies solution probability in sectors that contain feasible
covers and have positive invalid-state separation; unlike the global
statement, it necessarily contains the sector's kinetic floor.  Exact orbit
quotients expose inter-sector coincidences that close a global multiplicity
gap while remaining dark to the symmetric protocol; a stability theorem shows that
transverse dark crossings persist under small symmetry-preserving
perturbations.  For an
even-cycle family, the original linear interpolation has an exact global
multiplicity closure with a constant gap in the joint fixed sector.  We also give an
explicit Johnson/Metropolis parent-Hamiltonian path with a Dicke ground state
at one endpoint and cover probability $1-O(n^{-5})$ at the other.  A
zero-range comparison reduces its
accessible-gap problem to a three-box heat-bath chain.  A log-concave
coupling proves the uniform bound
$\Delta_{\rm acc,min}=\Omega(n^{-13})$; exact quotients display much larger
finite-size gaps but are not used as an asymptotic proof.  In the abstract
Hamiltonian-evolution model, derivative bounds also give a polynomial
adiabatic runtime, conditional on Dicke-state preparation and Hamiltonian
access.  The resulting
obstruction is conditional, not an
initial-state-independent no-go: a fixed closed Hamiltonian cannot relax,
and preserved symmetries forbid transitions between sectors, but a
different state preparation or a symmetry-breaking generator changes the
accessible spectrum.  We make no quantum-speedup claim; the contribution is
a rigorous separation of global, symmetry-allowed, and dynamically
accessible spectral structure.
\end{abstract}
