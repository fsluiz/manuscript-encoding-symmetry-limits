\section{Numerical protocol and finite-instance reconstruction}
\label{sec:numerical-reconstruction}

The numerical reconstruction follows the mathematical distinctions above.
Inputs are frozen with instance identifiers and SHA-256 hashes.  Every
headline gap is computed either in the full Hamiltonian or in an explicitly
labelled exact orbit quotient; projected $PHP$, Löwdin, and full-Hamiltonian
numbers are never placed in one unlabeled column.  A numerical degeneracy is
accepted only relative to eigenpair residuals and matrix scale.  Inside a
degenerate manifold, irrep content is obtained from
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{rank}(P_0\Pi_{\alpha\beta}P_0),
\end{equation}
not by assigning labels to solver-dependent individual eigenvectors.

Tables~\ref{tab:finite-structure} and \ref{tab:finite-endpoints} implement
this protocol on eleven frozen, unweighted instances.  Eight rows are
subinstances extracted from the OR-Library Set Cover benchmarks
\cite{Beasley1990,ORLib}; their parent indices, extraction metadata, and
SHA-256 hashes are stored in the instance registry.  Only the $2\times4$
grid has nontrivial $G_B$ (namely $D_4$); all other rows nevertheless retain
the $S_k$ reduction.  Five rows satisfy the strict inclusion
$\mathcal K_u\subsetneq\mathcal H_{\rm sym}$, so cyclic inaccessibility is
not merely a padding artefact.

At the problem endpoint, every accessible ground state is rank one in the
tested sample and
\begin{equation}
 p_{\rm opt}^{\min}\geq0.9944.
\end{equation}
Nine rows have $\delta_{\rm acc}>0$: their uniform symmetric protocol ends
above the global ground energy.  In the other two rows,
$\delta_{\rm acc}=0$ but the accessible excitation gap is orders of
magnitude larger than the full gap.  Thus neither equality nor inequality
of the endpoint ground energies determines which spectral gap is relevant.

For the sparse full-space calculations, the central class sum of register
transpositions separates every $S_k$ irrep for $k\leq5$.  The orbit of a
lowest vector constructs its complete Specht multiplet; deflation then
tests for further cospectral copies before $g_0^{\rm full}$ is assigned.
For the grid row the two-dimensional global ground manifold is additionally
the $E$ irrep of $D_4$, whereas the accessible branch is $A_1$.  At the
analytic hopping-cancellation point, the joint-fixed ground rank agrees
with the independently enumerated number of $G_B$-orbits of optimal covers
in every row.  Hence the original linear path has a fixed-sector rank
closure whenever this orbit count exceeds one.

The largest orbit-invariance residual is below $5\times10^{-13}$, the
largest cyclic-invariance residual below $2\times10^{-9}$, and all
full-space eigenpair and class-sum residuals used for reported levels are
stored in the machine-readable output.  The table values never combine
projected $PHP$, Löwdin, and full-Hamiltonian estimates.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:rewrite-conclusion}

For a fixed initial state and a symmetry-preserving interpolation, the
physically relevant spectrum is neither the full spectrum nor, in general,
the entire group-fixed spectrum.  It is the spectrum of the Hamiltonian
restricted to the cyclic space generated by that protocol.  Thus
$\mathcal K_u\subseteq\mathcal H_{\rm sym}$ is the central distinction:
changing the initial state, the generated algebra, or a preserved symmetry
can change the accessible spectrum and therefore the applicable gap.  The
fixed-Hamiltonian no-go statement is correspondingly conditional, rather
than independent of state preparation.

Within this framework, the sector-resolved localization theorem certifies
cover-measurement probability while retaining the sector kinetic floor.
Exact orbit quotients separate ground multiplicity, excitation gaps,
isolation gaps, and accessibility costs, and expose coincidences between
irreducible sectors that are dark to the symmetric protocol.  On the
even-cycle family, the original interpolation has an exact global
multiplicity closure while the joint-fixed excitation gap remains constant.
A distinct Johnson/Metropolis parent path has a uniformly polynomial cyclic
gap, $\Omega(n^{-13})$, and consequently a conditional polynomial adiabatic
runtime in the abstract Hamiltonian-access model.

These results establish a structural separation, not a practical quantum
speedup.  The cycle instances are classically solvable, Dicke-state
preparation has a cost that must be counted, the Gibbs parent endpoint is not
the original problem Hamiltonian, and the address count
$k\lceil\log_2n_B\rceil$ is not a complete hardware resource estimate.  A
natural next step is a decorated family whose classical cover structure is
nontrivial while its joint orbit quotient, dark global branch, and
polynomial accessible gap remain stable under controlled
symmetry-preserving perturbations.
