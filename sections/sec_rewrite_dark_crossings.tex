\section{Dark crossings in exact symmetry quotients}
\label{sec:rewrite-crossings}

If $\Pi_a$ and $\Pi_b$ project onto inequivalent irreps of a symmetry
preserved by the entire path, then
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_a\,\partial_sH(s)\,\Pi_b=0.
 \label{eq:dark-coupling-rewrite}
\end{equation}
An equality of the two branch energies is therefore a global crossing but
not an avoided crossing seen by a state confined to either block.

This phenomenon is stable within the symmetry-preserving class; it is not
restricted to exactly solvable diagonal cancellation points.

\begin{theorem}[Stability of a transverse dark crossing]
\label{thm:dark-crossing-stability}
Let $H_\varepsilon(s)=H(s)+\varepsilon V(s)$ be a continuously
differentiable finite-dimensional path, and let $\Pi_a,\Pi_b$ project onto
inequivalent symmetry sectors.  Suppose every $H_\varepsilon(s)$ commutes
with both projectors.  Assume that near $(s_\star,0)$ the two restricted
blocks have simple isolated eigenvalue branches $E_a(s,\varepsilon)$ and
$E_b(s,\varepsilon)$ such that
\begin{equation}
 E_a(s_\star,0)=E_b(s_\star,0),
 \qquad
 \partial_s(E_a-E_b)(s_\star,0)\ne0.
 \label{eq:transverse-dark-hypothesis}
\end{equation}
Then, for all sufficiently small $|\varepsilon|$, there is a unique nearby
$s_\star(\varepsilon)=s_\star+O(\varepsilon)$ at which the two branches
cross exactly, and
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_a\partial_sH_\varepsilon(s)\Pi_b=0.
\end{equation}
If the $a$ branch is separated from the rest of its block by at least
$\Delta_\star>0$ throughout a neighbourhood of $s_\star$ at
$\varepsilon=0$, then its isolation gap remains at least
$\Delta_\star/2$ whenever
$|\varepsilon|\sup_s\|V(s)\|\leq\Delta_\star/4$ and the perturbed crossing
stays in that neighbourhood.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Simple isolated eigenvalues of a differentiable Hermitian family define
differentiable local branches.  Apply the implicit-function theorem to
$F(s,\varepsilon)=E_a(s,\varepsilon)-E_b(s,\varepsilon)$ and use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:transverse-dark-hypothesis}.  Commutation with the symmetry
projectors makes every inter-sector matrix element vanish, so the equality
cannot become an avoided crossing.  Finally, Weyl's bound moves each
eigenvalue of the $a$ block by at most
$|\varepsilon|\|V(s)\|$; an internal spectral distance can therefore shrink
by at most twice this quantity, proving the stated gap bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite joint-symmetry audit}

For the frozen $2\times4$ incidence instance with $k=3$, the faithful base
group is $G_B\simeq D_4$ and the $S_3\times D_4$ fixed quotient has dimension
$22$, compared with $512$ in the valid ordered space.  Cyclic closure from
$\ket u$ also has dimension $22$; this equality is an output of the closure
calculation, not an assumption based on symmetry.  The orbit-isometry,
endpoint-invariance, and cyclic-invariance residuals are below
$10^{-13}$.

The uncatalysed linear path has a cancellation point at $s=7/15$ where the
fixed-space ground rank becomes two.  Thus this example is also a warning:
joint symmetry alone does not guarantee a nonzero accessible rank-one gap.
For this finite diagnostic, let
\begin{equation}
 W=\frac{\bm 1\bm 1^{\mathsf T}-I}{n_B-1},
 \qquad
 C_2=-\sum_{1\leq r<t\leq k}W^{(r)}W^{(t)},
 \label{eq:d4-catalyst-operator}
\end{equation}
and use the endpoint-preserving path
\begin{equation}
 H_\gamma(s)=(1-s)H_{\rm init}+sH_{\rm prob}
 +4\gamma s(1-s)C_2.
 \label{eq:d4-catalyst-path}
\end{equation}
The operator $C_2$ commutes with both $S_k$ and $G_B$, and its envelope
vanishes at $s=0,1$.  We set $\gamma=2$.  This catalyst moves the remaining
global crossing to $s\simeq0.806454$.  At that point the two branches belong
to different $D_4$ irreps,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dark-coupling-rewrite} has residual
$4.7\times10^{-14}$, and the $D_4$-trivial gap is approximately $0.258$.
The trivial-sector gap was evaluated on a uniform 2001-point grid and then
locally minimized within every grid bracket; its minimum is $0.0414$ at
$s\simeq0.874587$.  The crossing between the trivial floor and the
orthogonal-complement floor was bracketed on 401 points and refined by a
scalar root solve.  These figures are finite numerical diagnostics with
residual-based degeneracy tests, not interval-certified bounds, and they are
not used as an asymptotic theorem.

\subsection{An exact crossing on the even-cycle family}

The asymptotic witness is analytic.  Express minimum vertex cover on the
even cycle $C_n$, $n=2k$, as set cover: bases are vertices, tasks are edges,
and each base covers its two incident edges.  The only minimum covers are
the two alternating subsets, which form one orbit of $D_n$.

Consider the original ordered-register linear path
\begin{equation}
 H(s)=(1-s)H_{\rm init}+sH_{\rm prob}.
 \label{eq:cycle-linear-rewrite}
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal coefficient of each single-register complete-graph move is
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{1-s}{n}+\frac{s}{n-1},
\end{equation}
and vanishes exactly at
\begin{equation}
 s_c=\frac{n-1}{2n-1}.
 \label{eq:cycle-cancel-rewrite}
\end{equation}
At this point the Hamiltonian is diagonal up to a scalar.  All $2k!$
ordered representatives of the two alternating covers are global ground
states, whereas their normalized orbit is the unique ground vector in the
$S_k\times D_n$ fixed space.  Assuming $\mu\geq\lambda$, the next fixed
orbit violates at least one coverage constraint, and
\begin{align}
 g_0^{\rm global}(s_c)&=2k!,&
 g_0^{\rm sym}(s_c)&=1,\\
 \Delta_{\rm sym}(s_c)&=\lambda\frac{n-1}{2n-1}.
 \label{eq:cycle-dark-crossing-rewrite}
\end{align}
Thus the global multiplicity gap closes exactly while the joint-fixed gap
is bounded below by a constant.  The crossing is dynamically dark for the
uniform initial state and the symmetry-preserving path.

Equation~\eqref{eq:cycle-dark-crossing-rewrite} controls the crossing point,
not the minimum gap on every part of the linear path.  The next section
introduces a different, explicit parent path on the same family and proves
a uniform polynomial bound.  Keeping these statements separate avoids
turning a pointwise crossing theorem into an unsupported adiabatic claim.
